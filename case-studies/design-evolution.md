# From an evidence observatory to a business scene

I want the visual design to explain what the product does. The earlier Observatory organized fictional evidence into cases, relationships and a review tray. Later versions explored an animated Cira presence, a restrained typographic layout and a photographic setting. Those designs remain preserved as distinct checkpoints rather than overwritten or presented as unrelated products.

The current retail direction gives the visitor a concrete sequence: enter a shop, inspect the product and follow an inquiry to a confirmed pickup request. The spatial setting supplies context while the stock card and staff tablet reveal the consequence of the decision.

These are actual saved design checkpoints, not evidence of customer adoption. The earlier revenue-integrity concept was a business hypothesis; the current scene is a broader integration demonstration. Neither has verified commercial results in this record.

A navigation issue exposed a useful implementation lesson: an animation-frame timestamp may precede the event that starts a route. Route sampling now clamps its endpoints, with boundary cases recorded in [verification](../verification/2026-11-18-retail.md). The next design step depends on reviewing the current single-business experience before expanding the street.
